GEARS XR Tutorial

Watch molecules move — in your browser, or in VR. No installation, no account, no experience needed.

Open the viewer → Free · runs in any modern browser
Quick start

Get started in three clicks

A demo molecule is already filled in for you, so your first visit takes about ten seconds.

  1. Open the viewer in your browser.
  2. Click Load URL in the top-left panel. The demo molecule loads in a moment.
  3. Click Play in the panel that appears at the bottom-left. That's it — the atoms start moving.
Orientation

The window at a glance

Everything lives in four small panels around the edges — the molecule always has center stage.

Main panel (top-left) opens files and changes the scenery. Enter VR (top-right) starts headset mode. Playback (bottom-left) plays the motion. Room (bottom-right) lets you watch together with others. The Main and Room panels each have a Hide button to tuck them away.

You can also look around freely: drag to orbit around the molecule, scroll to zoom, and right-drag to slide sideways.

Your own data

Open your own molecule

GEARS XR reads .xyz and .extxyz trajectories, plus Gaussian Cube files (.cube and .cub).

The easiest way: drag the file from your computer and drop it anywhere on the page. Or click Choose File in the main panel.

Got a file in the cloud? Paste a Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, or GitHub link into the URL box and click Load URL — share links are understood automatically. Just make sure the file is shared as "Anyone with the link."

Studying a known material? Type a Materials Project ID like mp-149 (silicon) into the URL box and click Load URL — GEARS XR fetches the crystal structure straight from the Materials Project. Pasting the material's web page link works too.

Local-file privacy: files you drop or choose stay on your device during solo use. If you are the presenter in a room, GEARS XR temporarily uploads the current file so the other room members can view it. Room uploads are limited to 50 MB, expire after 24 hours, and may be removed sooner if shared storage fills up.
Playback

Play the movie

If your file holds more than one snapshot, the playback panel turns it into a movie.

  1. Drag the slider to jump to any moment.
  2. Play / Pause runs the motion; < and > step one frame at a time.
  3. FPS sets the speed — higher numbers play faster.
Ambience

Change the scenery

Pick a 360° background from the Background menu in the main panel — it surrounds you completely in VR.

The default, Dark Cyberspace, keeps atom colors easiest to see. Choose None for a plain dark room.

Density & orbitals

See electron density and orbitals

Open a Gaussian Cube file (.cube) — the format chemistry programs export — to see the cloud of electron density or a molecular orbital as a smooth 3D surface around the atoms.

Load a .cube file the same way as any other — drag it onto the page, or paste its link and click Load URL. The atoms appear as usual, wrapped in a translucent surface. An Isosurface panel shows up at the bottom-left. It's a list of surfaces — start with the ones shown and click + Add surface to stack more (up to six). Each row is one surface you control on its own:

  1. Color — the swatch on the left recolors that surface; click it to pick any color.
  2. Isovalue — how much of the cloud that surface shows. Drag the slider, or type an exact value in the box below it — handy for a precise level like 0.002. Toward zero grows the surface; outward shrinks it to the densest core. A negative value draws the opposite orbital lobe.
  3. Opacity — how see-through it is, so inner surfaces and the atoms still show through.
  4. Checkbox hides a surface; × removes it.

Stacking a few — say a solid inner core and a faint outer shell at a smaller isovalue — shows how the density falls off with distance. For an orbital, keep a blue positive surface and a red one at the negative isovalue. The Reset button restores the starting surfaces if you want to begin again.

Try it: load samples/orbital.cube (bundled with the viewer) for a sample orbital. A cube is a single snapshot, so there's no play timeline — just the surfaces. It works in VR too.
Analysis

Measure atoms with clicks

Curious how far apart two atoms are, or how sharp an angle is? Just click them.

  1. Click two atoms → the distance between them appears.
  2. Click a third atom → the angle at the middle atom appears.
  3. Press C to clear and start a new measurement.
This works in VR too — point a controller at an atom and pull the trigger.
Collaboration

Watch together

Explore the same molecule with a colleague, a classroom, or a friend — all you share is a short room code.

  1. Load a molecule with Load URL, Choose File, or drag and drop. If it is a local file, GEARS XR shares it automatically after you join as the presenter.
  2. Click Show on the Room panel at the bottom-right, then Join.
  3. Click Copy Code and send the short code to the others — chat, email, or out loud.
  4. They type the same code and click Join. The structure, playback, view, background, and isosurfaces follow the presenter.

The first person in the room is the presenter — they steer playback, the view, the molecule, the background, and any isosurfaces. Anyone else can click Take Presenter to take the wheel. Atom measurement selections stay private to each browser.

Virtual reality

Step inside with VR

With a VR headset (like a Meta Quest), the molecule floats in the room with you.

  1. Put on your headset and open the viewer URL in its browser.
  2. Load a molecule, then tap Enter VR at the top-right.

Grab with one hand (hold the trigger) to move and turn the molecule. Grab with both hands and pull them apart or together to make it bigger or smaller — like stretching a photo on a phone.

Don't see the Enter VR button light up? Your current browser or device doesn't support VR — everything else on this page still works on a normal screen.
Help

If something looks wrong

The most common hiccups, and their one-line fixes.

"This URL returned a web page, not a data file"

Your cloud share link isn't public yet. In Google Drive/Dropbox/OneDrive, set the file to "Anyone with the link" and try again.

"Expected a .xyz, .extxyz, .cube, or .cub file"

The link points at a file with a different extension. Rename it with one of the supported extensions, or use a direct link to the actual data file.

My friend joined the room but sees no molecule

If you loaded a local file, wait for Shared with the room to appear in the status line. Files larger than 50 MB cannot be shared through a room; use a public URL instead.

The room stays on "Connecting"

Check the Room server field — keep the default value unless someone gave you a different server address.

I can't move the slider or change the speed in a room

Only the presenter controls playback. Click Take Presenter first.

The VR button says "VR not supported"

Open the page in a VR headset's own browser (for example the Quest Browser). Regular desktop browsers usually can't enter VR.
